            How to spot template work

            Generic AI templates are the new snake oil these days: swap the logo and the colors and it's a plumber, a restaurant or an agency. Here's one I built for a plumber I made up as an example.

            Almost every agency running ads in my feed cites something like this as their... "work." I'll be scrolling Instagram, and half the time their ads aren't even showing fully - so automated they didn't notice the ad they paid for is missing the top 30% of the image.

            These are things to watch out for when sizing up website work - but they generally apply to everything, like strategy, content, flyers, and so on:

            • A heading with one word italicized or recolored for no clear reason, especially if every heading does it.
            • Generic reassurance copy like "no credit card required" stacked under every button.
            • Case studies and results for client sites that don't exist: click through and the "clients" are the same template with different names, or nothing comes up when you search them.
            • The same narrow palette everywhere: dark-mode backgrounds, purple or blue accents, endless rounded-corner cards.
            • Copy that reads the same everywhere: the same sentence rhythm, the same "in today's fast-paced world" openers, whatever the topic.
            • An Instagram or Facebook ad selling websites from a business with no website of its own: nothing linked in the ad, nothing when you search their name.

            And my personal favorites:

            • "We'll build your site in 2 hours"
            • "We'll build your site for [insert suspiciously low price]"
            • "We'll build your site free, and if you don't like it, you don't pay for it"

            Ask yourself, is that quality of work worth paying for, just to end up looking like everyone else who got the same pitch?
