Real work vs template work

A good template, built on real code and properly customized, is smart money. The one to watch for is the generic AI version: swap the logo and the colors and it's a plumber, a restaurant or an agency. I check every agency that runs an ad in my feed, and about one in four is selling a site like this, complete with results for clients that don't exist. Here's one I made for a plumbing business that doesn't exist.

Whether you're sizing up a website, a proposal or a piece of content, look for these:

  • A heading with one word italicized or recolored for no clear reason, especially if every heading does it.
  • The same narrow palette everywhere: dark-mode backgrounds, purple or blue accents, endless rounded-corner cards.
  • Generic reassurance copy like "no credit card required" stacked under every button.
  • Copy that reads the same everywhere: the same sentence rhythm, the same "in today's fast-paced world" openers, whatever the topic.
  • Case studies and results for client sites that don't exist: click through and the "clients" are the same template with different names, or nothing comes up when you search them.
  • An Instagram or Facebook ad selling websites from a business with no website of its own: nothing linked in the ad, nothing when you search their name.
